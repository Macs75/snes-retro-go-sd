# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **SA-1 support.** Kirby Super Star, Kirby's Dream Land 3 and Super Mario RPG
  boot and play. The chip is ZSNES's; its CPU is the core's own 65816, run a
  second time, and on device a Thumb-2 engine of its own. Its code is loaded
  only for an SA-1 cart, and its BW-RAM is the size the cart's header gives
  (8 KB Kirby Super Star, 32 KB Mario RPG and Kirby's Dream Land 3); the
  128 KB carts are not attached yet. On device, Kirby Super Star's intro and
  attract run at 96% of real time on average, most scenes at full speed.

### Performance

- **SA-1: proven wait loops are slept through**, and the 65816's idle skip now
  takes loops that poll SA-1 memory while the SA-1 sleeps; both CPUs read ROM
  and BW-RAM through page maps. Kirby Super Star's intro and attract on device:
  ~50% → 84% of real time. Machine state identical to running every lap.
- **SA-1: the two CPUs meet only where they touch.** The SA-1 catches up when
  the 65816 reads or writes something it shares (I-RAM, BW-RAM, its registers,
  its vectors) and at the frame's events, instead of before every 65816
  instruction; the 65816 reaches I-RAM and BW-RAM without a call.
- **SA-1: its own Thumb-2 65816** (`thumb2/sa1_engine.S`, generated by
  `tools/gen_sa1_engine.py`). It keeps A, X, Y, the flags, the pc as a pointer
  into the ROM page and the clocks in registers for a whole slice, with a
  dispatch table per register width; INX is 9 ARM instructions instead of 68.
  23 KB: the 123 opcodes with memory operands seen in the three games, the rest
  in C. Kirby Super Star's intro and attract on device (replay of a recording):
  87.8% → 95.7% of real time against the main CPU's engine assembled for the
  SA-1, late frames 59% → 38%, audio dropouts 15.0 s → 4.9 s; the SA-1
  overlay 127 → 65 KB. The replay's state hashes equal the host's.
- **SA-1: the 65816's waits for the SA-1 are skipped while it works.** The
  idle skip takes loops polling `$2300`/`$2301` (the SA-1's message, its IRQ
  flags) too, finds the loop the 65816 is in between NMIs, and while the SA-1
  runs advances a lap after bringing the SA-1 to that lap's read and finding
  the same bytes. Kirby Super Star on device (PERF replay): heavy windows
  132% → 115% of the frame budget at worst, the 65816's share 51-59% → 41-44%;
  the replay's state hashes equal the host's.
- **SA-1: it runs ahead while the 65816 waits for its message.** When the
  65816 polls only `$2300`/`$2301`, the SA-1 runs over all the laps in one go
  up to its first write that changes them, and the laps before it are skipped
  at once. Kirby Super Star on device (PERF replay): SA-1 engine calls in the
  heavy windows down a third to a half, those windows 107-118% → 101-109% of
  the budget, mean speed 98.0% → 99.4%; state hashes equal the host's.
- **Idle loops are skipped after a line's HDMA too.** A span that started in
  the HDMA stall was never offered to the idle skip; now the rest of it is,
  once the stall is taken. Kirby Super Star on device (PERF replay): windows
  over budget 9 → 4, the worst 109% → 104.5%. Final Fantasy III and Super
  Mario Kart skip more of their waits as well. Host replays of six games and
  gwemu runs of three give the same state hashes as before.
- **SA-1: the last, partial lap of a run-ahead is skipped too.** Its read is
  included in the run-ahead; with nothing changed, the partial lap is stepped
  through the proven lap's states instead of being run. Kirby Super Star on
  device (PERF replay): no window over budget any more (the worst 104.5% →
  99.0%), late frames 1,397 → 1,173. Host: Mario RPG's attract runs 5.5% fewer
  65816 instructions, Kirby's Dream Land 3's 0.4%, both with the same state
  every frame.
- **Idle skip: loops reading up to three SA-1 addresses, and `$4212` polls.**
  While the SA-1 works, the 65816's wait loop is now skipped lap by lap when
  it reads up to three addresses the SA-1 shares (one before), the SA-1
  brought to each read's dot in turn. And a loop polling `$4212` (vblank,
  hblank, auto-joypad busy) is skipped over a span in which that value holds.
  Super Mario RPG's intro and attract on device (PERF replay of a gameplay
  recording): windows over budget 59 → 54, over 110% 40 → 22, the worst
  140.8% → 130.7%, mean speed 93.5% → 96.1%, the 65816's instructions a third
  fewer; the replay's state hashes equal the host's.
- **SA-1: DMA from BW-RAM reads it directly, and the SA-1 engine covers more
  opcodes.** A DMA from BW-RAM banks `$40-$4F` brings the SA-1 up to date once
  and reads the bytes straight from memory, not one by one through the cart;
  MVN and MVP and 11 more opcode forms run in the Thumb-2 engine (+4.3 KB),
  so Mario RPG's SA-1 runs 0.6% of its instructions in C instead of 4.2%.
  Super Mario RPG on device (PERF replay): windows over budget 54 → 34, over
  120% 8 → 1, the worst 130.7% → 124.2%, mean speed 96.1% → 98.5%, DMA's share
  of the heavy windows 12.8% → 1.5%; state hashes unchanged.
- **SA-1: the 65816's waits on an SA-1 flag are found where the NMI never
  lands.** A new wait loop was only looked for where an NMI, an IRQ or a `$2300`
  read caught the CPU; Mario RPG waits for the SA-1 in a WRAM loop polling
  I-RAM `$3002` while its NMI always lands in another loop, so 3,552 laps a
  frame ran. Two spans in a row starting within a loop's length of each other
  now search for a loop there (8 searches a frame at most, SA-1 carts only),
  and arm it if it polls SA-1 memory. Host: Mario RPG's recording 13.1% fewer
  65816 instructions (its intro window 7,527 → 1,188 a frame), Kirby Super Star
  0.2%, no window heavier, the same state every frame.
- **SA-1 engine: fewer resyncs, BW-RAM writes inline.** REP/SEP/PLP resync
  only when they clear the I flag, writes to the SA-1's arithmetic, bit-reader
  and vector registers no longer resync, and writes to BW-RAM banks `$40-$4F`
  go straight to memory instead of through C. Counted on gwemu: 36.7 → 30.8 ARM
  instructions per SA-1 instruction. Super Mario RPG on device (PERF replay):
  engine cycles per SA-1 instruction 79.2 → 75.7, the SA-1's share 18.7% →
  18.0%, no window heavier; state hashes unchanged.
- **The new SA-1 wait-loop search no longer costs where it finds nothing.** It
  ran its 8 searches a frame in scenes without such a loop and failed each
  time: now it searches only at a pc a recent span already started at, and
  remembers the pcs it rejected. Super Mario RPG on device (PERF replay):
  windows over budget 34 → 29, the worst 116.0% → 113.1%, mean speed 99.0%,
  late frames 7,777 → 7,390, no window heavier.
- **Idle skip: a `$4212` wait is skipped in both halves of the line.** `BIT
  $4212` copies the h-blank bit into V, so a lap in the other half of the line
  is another lap, and a third of the spans of Mario RPG's vblank wait ran in
  full. The loop now keeps a proof for each value it reads (one site at a
  time, the second proof in DTCM), a lap whose first read leaves a stale flag
  is proven one lap later, and a span that starts on such a flag runs one lap
  before it is skipped instead of spending a proof. While the SA-1 works, a
  loop that reads none of its memory is skipped as usual. Host, ten
  recordings: Mario RPG 42.8% fewer 65816 instructions, Kirby Super Star 1.5%,
  Star Fox 1.2%, no window heavier in any, the same state every frame. Super
  Mario RPG on device (PERF replay, 60 fps): windows over budget 29 → 24, over
  110% 6 → 2, the worst 113.1% → 111.2%, late frames 7,452 → 6,777, 582 more
  frames drawn; state hashes unchanged.
- **SA-1: a run-ahead also covers a wait on I-RAM, and loops with up to three
  SA-1 reads.** It was taken only by a loop with one read of `$2300`/`$2301`;
  now the SA-1 also runs ahead while the 65816 polls I-RAM bytes, up to the
  first write that changes one of them. The engine watches I-RAM only during
  a run-ahead: it switches to a second set of its handlers, the 75 that store
  to I-RAM with the check, so the rest of the time runs the same code as
  before; the C interpreter looks once after each opcode, and a write to a
  register that cannot change what is watched skips the check. Super Mario
  RPG on device (PERF replay, 60 fps): windows over budget 24 → 14, under
  20 fps 8 → 0, the lowest 13.5 → 25.2 fps, 1,484 more frames drawn, the
  65816's share 4.7 points and the SA-1's 3.4 points lower; state hashes
  unchanged.
- **SA-1: its hot code sits at fixed I-cache offsets.** The SA-1's C bus
  helpers and span runner start an 8 KB-aligned window in its overlay, in a
  fixed order, and its Thumb-2 engine follows at a fixed offset, so a change
  elsewhere in the core no longer moves them. Kirby Super Star on device
  (PERF replay): windows over budget 4 → 1, the SA-1's share 0.7 points lower
  on average (its heaviest window 39.3% → 36.5% of the budget), late frames
  1,376 → 1,285; state hashes unchanged.
- **The SA-1 wait-loop search and the known-site lookup cost less.** The
  search remembers its rejects in 16 sets of 4 instead of 64 single slots:
  pcs of one wait loop that hashed to the same slot evicted each other, and
  77% of Kirby Super Star's searches, ~24K cycles each, were of pcs it had
  already rejected. On every span outside the active site, the look through
  the known sites and the search's first test now run in ITCM in one call,
  checking each site's range first, instead of two calls into RAM code (~290
  cycles a span, up to ~800 spans a frame). Kirby Super Star on device (PERF
  replay): the 65816's share 0.7 points lower on average, its heaviest
  windows 2.4 points lower (39.2% → 36.8% of the budget), late frames
  1,285 → 1,188, and now below the build without the search. Super Mario RPG
  (60 fps): the 65816's share 0.5 points lower, 98 more frames drawn, the
  lowest 24.1 → 24.4 fps. Host: the same state and instruction counts in all
  ten recordings.

## [v0.0.8]

### Added

- **Super FX (GSU) support.** Yoshi's Island, Star Fox, Doom, Vortex, Dirt Trax
  FX, Stunt Race FX and Winter Gold boot and play. The GSU core is the ZSNES
  one, made to fit the G&W, and is loaded only for a Super FX cart. Yoshi's
  Island and Star Fox run their hot GSU code as precompiled blocks, each game's
  loaded only for its own cart. Yoshi's title screen went from 156% to 87% of
  the frame budget on device, gameplay averages under it and the heaviest room
  (the waterfall) sits at it; Star Fox's title runs at full speed and its intro
  and gameplay went from ~1.65x to ~1.25x real time; its mission map and planet
  zoom, up to 2.7x, now run at full speed. Save states work for Super
  FX carts; one saved by an earlier build is refused.
- Diagnostics: `PERF_SPC=1` (the SPC700 time split), host `APU_HIST`,
  `CPU_PCHIST` and `IDLE_TRACE`, the GSU spike/coverage and core memory reports
  in `tools/`, `tools/state_resume_test.py` (a save state resumed in a fresh
  process must continue like the run that saved it), host `DMA_HIST` with
  `tools/dma_hist_report.py`, and `tools/replay_ab.py` (two host builds must
  replay recordings to the same machine state).

### Performance

- **SPC700: the Thumb-2 engine runs whole batches of opcodes**, instead of being
  called once per opcode from C. Generic, every game's sound driver runs through
  it. Yoshi's Island waterfall room: SPC700 507K → 321K cycles/frame, an
  estimated 2.5% of the frame budget against a normal build. Output
  bit-identical (Castlevania and Yoshi's Island replays on the Cortex-M7
  harness).
- **DMA without the per-byte bus decode and register dispatch.** The source is
  read directly (plain memory through the CPU's page map: WRAM and its mirror,
  LoROM and Super FX ROM; and the Super FX Game Pak RAM), uploads to VRAM,
  CGRAM and OAM are written as blocks into the PPU's data ports, and a VRAM
  word upload writes each low/high pair as one word. Generic. Yoshi's Island
  waterfall room: DMA 562K → 191K cycles/frame, busy 100% → 94% of the budget,
  late frames halved. Output bit-identical (Super Mario Kart,
  Castlevania, Batman, TMNT IV and Yoshi's Island replays).
- **Offset-per-tile backgrounds** (BG modes 2 and 4 with OPT, Star Fox's 3D
  scenes) decode each tile once instead of every pixel bit by bit, skip blank
  tiles and read BG3's offset table once per pass. Star Fox on device: PPU
  41-43% → 24-26% of the frame budget, intro and gameplay from 1.25x to
  ~1.07x real time. Output bit-identical.
- **Idle skip: Super FX status polls** (`LDA $3030 / AND #imm / BNE`) are
  skipped while only the GSU flags the loop doesn't test change. Star Fox on
  device: gameplay CPU -14%, busy 103% → 97% of the budget, audio underruns
  over the replay -56%. Output bit-identical.

### Changed

- **Audio goes straight to the output**, without the stretcher
  (`SNES_AUDIO_DIRECT=1`, the default; 0 restores it). The frame loop keeps
  three frames of sound queued beyond the one playing: a heavy frame plays from
  the queue and the frames after it refill it. Music never changes pitch or
  tempo; where the emulator is slower than real time for longer than the queue
  covers, the sound has short gaps instead of slowing down. ~0.6% of the frame
  budget less.
- **Idle skip:** a wait loop entered in another CPU environment (M/X/E, DB, DP)
  gets a site of its own instead of replacing the existing one (8 sites, was 4),
  and the site search also runs at IRQ requests, so loops that wait for an IRQ
  mid-frame are found.
- The Cx4, S-DD1, SPC7110 and DSP-1 code joined the Super FX core as overlays
  loaded only for their carts, and the private framebuffer holds only the
  256x240 picture: ~65 KB more RAM for every game.

### Fixed

- The auto-joypad read's busy flag (`$4212` bit 0) now sets for ~3 lines at
  the start of vblank; it never did. Star Fox hung on the mission briefing,
  which waits for it before reading the pad.
- PROFLITE no longer reports the audio as slowed for the stretcher's normal
  ±1% trimming, only below 0.99x.

## [v0.0.7]

A targeted follow-up: a generic sub-screen compositing skip that cuts
colour-math-heavy scenes, a platform-invariant APU catch-up so host and device
replays stay bit-for-bit identical, one more idle-skip template, and two
record/replay and host-input fixes.

### Performance

- **PPU sub-screen skip.** The sub-screen is composited every line while colour
  math is on, but the blend reads it only where a main-screen pixel sits on a
  colour-math-enabled layer. A per-line scan of the main z-buffer
  (`PpuLineReadsSubscreen`) skips building the sub-screen when no such pixel
  exists — `ClearBackdrop` has already left it a valid empty buffer, so those
  lines take the no-math fast composite and the output is byte-identical. The
  scan is gated behind active colour math and early-exits on the first match, so
  a plain scene never runs it and a full-screen-translucency scene pays about one
  iteration. The Adventures of Batman & Robin enables OBJ-only colour math but
  its visible sprites are non-math palettes, so the sub-screen is built and never
  read: sub build ~855K -> ~0 cycles/frame, the blend span goes with it, PPU
  total 2.05M -> 1.39M (-32%), the heavy frame off the budget ceiling (96% ->
  79%). Neutral where the sub-screen is genuinely read — Super Punch-Out's 600K
  spikes and Zelda's rain are unchanged. Device-verified; output byte-identical
  across Batman, TMNT, Castlevania and Zelda.

### Added

- **The Adventures of Batman & Robin idle-skip template.** An 80:80ed bare
  accumulator. Unlike the earlier templates it has no in-loop TCD, so the direct
  page is ambient rather than forced; a new per-template DP-guard field gates the
  skip on `cpu->dp` (re-checked each frame) so its WRAM offsets apply only in the
  context they were measured in.
- **International Superstar Soccer (+ Deluxe) idle-skip templates.** Konami's
  Dracula-shape accumulator (in-loop TCD forces DP=0) at `80:80ef` (ISS) and
  `80:80d4` (Deluxe); the two share identical loop bytes, so the head tells them
  apart and the 16-byte guard disambiguates either from the other. Each entry
  covers USA and EUR (byte-identical there). On device, Deluxe is the dominant
  idle wait across the attract/demo (~92k dots/frame skipped): cpu −16%, busy
  −12%, PPU/APU unchanged, worst late window 37→16. (ISS host-verified only.)
- **The Legend of the Mystical Ninja idle-skip template (USA + EUR).** The TMNT
  JSL+accumulator shape at `00:80b4`; the per-lap `JSL $009d27` (USA) / `$009d3e`
  (EUR, byte-identical routine) early-returns writing nothing when `[$001e2c]==0`,
  so the skip carries the same precondition as TMNT. Host-verified bit-exact over
  the attract (both regions) and an 11660-frame USA gameplay recording. It
  reclaims the title/menu/idle phases; in active gameplay the loop busy-waits with
  per-lap JSL work, so the skip is mostly inactive there (that CPU is real work).

### Changed

- **Deterministic APU catch-up.** The owed-APU-cycle accumulation in
  `snes_catchupApu` moved from a `double` — which x86 (host) and ARM (device
  Thumb-2) rounded differently, occasionally tipping the truncation by one cycle
  and desyncing the SPC700 between the two — to an integer Q32 fixed-point
  accumulator with an exact rational cycles-per-master-dot (NTSC 2136/44671, PAL
  1068/22165). Byte-identical rate, platform-invariant: host and device replays
  now match bit-for-bit, which every A/B in this project depends on. Same struct
  size, so the savestate layout is unchanged.
- Host save-state keys are now **F2 (save) / F4 (load)**.

### Fixed

- A failed or invalid state load (wrong state version, truncated file) no longer
  kills an in-progress record/replay: record/replay is closed only after the
  loaded state passes validation, so a bad load leaves the replay running.

## [v0.0.6]

The performance release. A generic idle-loop skip, a page-mapped 65816 bus, a
batch-entry Thumb-2 engine and a reworked PPU together cut frameskip across the
library, and a new deterministic record/replay system was used to verify every
change bit-for-bit.

### Added

- **Record & replay.** Record the controller from power-on and replay it
  deterministically (device pause menu, or on the host / QEMU Cortex-M7 target),
  with per-frame state and audio hashes. Host-made recordings replay
  frame-identically on the device, which is how the optimisations below were
  A/B'd bit-exactly.
- **On-device profiling.** `make PERF=1` cycle profiler; `PERF_CPU=1` breaks the
  65816 cost down by opcode (census runnable on the QEMU Cortex-M7 target);
  `PROFLITE=1` prints a cheap per-frame frameskip/audio report; the PPU
  compositing cost is split into `cwin` / `span` / `other`.
- **Per-game idle-skip templates, with a library-wide survey.** On top of the
  generic skip (below), a guarded template table (`src/snes/idle_skip.c`) covers
  the few wait loops the generic detector can't prove — accumulators (`x += y`
  every lap) and `$4212` H/V polls. Each template is matched on the exact ROM
  bytes, so it can only ever fire on the ROM it describes; a new one is added
  once its loop is found and its skip is verified bit-exact. The overnight
  survey that classifies the wait loop of 134 top games (which already skip,
  which need a generic fix, which need a template), a contributor guide, and the
  survey tooling itself are in `docs/idle-skip.md`, `docs/snes_idle_survey.*` and
  `tools/idle-survey/`.
- **Unthrottled host replays** (`SNES_FRAMES=N`, or `SNES_FAST=1`) — the host
  build drops its two wall-clock pacing gates and runs a headless replay flat
  out (~11x, a 2000-frame replay in ~2.9 s), so a multi-game bit-exact A/B takes
  seconds instead of ~30 minutes. Host-only; it touches only the wall-clock
  waits, so the emulated state/audio hash is unchanged and the device bin is
  unaffected.
- `docs/perf-lessons.md` documenting the core's performance lessons.

### Performance

Frameskip reductions are per-scene, measured on the device from deterministic
replays:

- **Generic idle-loop skip** — detects the main wait loop at the NMI and replays
  whole idle laps in one step; covers many loop shapes ($4210 / $4212 polls,
  accumulators, DP/DB changes, WRAM code, SEP/REP, indexed). Around 60% of
  surveyed games skip about half of the frame.
- **Page-mapped 65816 bus** — WRAM and ROM are served through an 8 KB page map
  instead of the per-access classifier; a HiROM low-half fast path cut the
  Final Fantasy VI intro frameskip by ~62%.
- **HDMA stall taken in one step** — after a line's HDMA transfers the CPU waits
  out the stall; the scheduler stepped it 2 dots at a time (~26,500 do-nothing
  calls/frame in Final Fantasy VI, which runs HDMA every line). Taken whole:
  FF VI busy 95.3% -> 82.8%, late frames 3911 -> 1659.
- **Thumb-2 engine** — a batch-entry `run_dots` loop keeps the dot-timing state
  in registers; cold code and the I/O slow path moved out of ITCM.
- **Fixed, cache-line-aligned code layout.** The hot code (SPC700 engine, DSP,
  PPU line renderer, 65816 bus and C fallback) is pinned in fixed-size,
  32-byte-aligned windows in the linker script, and the Thumb-2 65816 engine at
  a fixed ITCM address, so a change to one group can't shift another's
  instruction-cache sets. Before this, an edit touching only `dsp.c` moved the
  SPC700 engine and swung it ~25k cycles/frame, and bus/PPU set-aliasing cost
  the CPU ~67k cycles/frame — both now designed out, and builds are comparable.
- **PPU renderer** — Mode 7 fast paths for non-rotated lines and a split-out
  tile-cell cache for rotated / large-field lines (Super Mario Kart ~-53%);
  2bpp/4bpp full-tile run functions with a 256-entry row-decode table and
  per-repeat tile-row reuse (4bpp layers -15% in heavy scenes); colour-math
  blend spans rewritten as memoised, adaptively-ordered span functions (inlined
  they ran out of registers and reloaded the blend-table address every pixel —
  the same register-pressure trap as the Mode 7 split; composite 598K -> 437K);
  sprite slivers skip transparent rows (-21%); and the palette and colour-math
  tables are refreshed only for the CGRAM entries that changed, not rebuilt whole
  every line — in gradient/HDMA scenes the table work drops 333K -> 25K
  (PPU -306K/frame).
- **Memory** — cart SRAM and the coprocessor / idle-skip buffers (~48 KB) moved
  from the RAM_EMU pool to the cached AHB heap. This speeds the DMA that writes
  them (into cached SRAM, ~-20% on steady frames) and, because it hands the pool
  back to the hot data, also speeds the 65816 CPU work that stayed there even
  though it barely touches SRAM (Zelda cpu -3.8% vs busy -2.6%). Late frames
  collapse in heavy scenes: Zelda 89 -> 31, Castlevania 140 -> 46.
- **APU CPU time** — the three SPC700 timers advance lazily (closed-form, only
  where the sound program can observe them) instead of stepping after every
  opcode: SPC700 -15% (882K -> 748K cycles/frame), heaviest frame -362K. The
  DSP's idle-voice skip (released voices caught up in one step) is on by default
  (-85K/frame in quiet scenes). Both only became measurable once the code was
  pinned (see the layout note) — unpinned, placement noise hid them.
- **Audio playback quality** — the stretcher now derives its playback rate
  correctly across uneven ISR refills (a ratio of sums, the refill pulled in one
  chunk, folded over the two-frame half-rate pair), so the rate holds 1.0x
  instead of swinging per refill. See the matching Fixed entry for what this
  cured.
- **Display scaling** — the FULL and FIT present modes were rewritten to map
  256x224 into the 320x240 panel with no per-pixel division and 32-bit stores;
  pixel-identical output at ~154k cycles per shown frame (from ~560k).

### Changed

- Structural cleanup of the per-frame hot paths (DMA fast paths, `snes_write`
  early-returns, folded colour-math mask) — bit-identical output, fewer cycles.

### Fixed

- Final Fantasy III (USA) no longer hangs on a black screen at startup: a
  `$4210` (NMI flag) read now sees a vblank that begins inside the reading
  instruction.
- Music no longer plays flat or drops audio while the emulator is keeping time.
  The stretcher's playback-rate estimate was wrong across uneven ISR refills, so
  e.g. Zelda's title played ~3 semitones flat and threw away 12-16% of the sound
  to stay in sync. (Emulation output is unchanged — this is the playback path.)

### Install

- Unzip the release archive onto the SD card root (`cores/snes.bin`).
- Place ROMs under `/roms/snes/`
